I don’t think there’s enough optimism or positive energy around video games these days, and I get why. It’s hard out there on all fronts, from indie games to AAA to journalism.

But today, I had a great day.

A couple of great conversations about video games refuelled my creative engines.

## I Bought Ocarina of Time on Launch Day for the Second Time

![A tabby cat lies beside a boxed copy of The Legend of Zelda: Ocarina of Time for Nintendo Switch 2.](./IMG_3804%20Medium.jpeg)

The remake of [*The Legend of Zelda: Ocarina of Time*][zelda] came out today, and it’s been lovely watching the midnight launches online.

As for me, I didn’t realize today was the day. I was sure it was Sunday.

Maybe because I’m usually busy with the day job and family. Maybe because I’m burning out.

Either way, it eventually sank in that my pre-order was ready for pickup today. I’d planned to pick it up on Sunday. I do my video game shopping here in Winnipeg at [PnP Games][pnp]. They started the [Don’t Kill the Disc: Tell Sony to Keep Physical PlayStation Games][petition] petition on Change.org.

The PnP team does some very cool things, like hosting the [midnight launch for *Ocarina of Time*][launch]. Like sponsoring [classic episodes of *Electric Playground*][ep-classic] on YouTube. Like making [commercials][pnp-commercials] that feel right at home in those old episodes.

They don’t have to do any of that, but they do.

They also brought in Victor Lucas from EPNtv and *Electric Playground* for the midnight launch. He hung out at stores around the city while folks came to pick up their new Zelda game.

And so, after another tough day at the day job, my partner and I sorted out the kiddos’ after-school activities so I could head over to pick up my game and hopefully meet Victor Lucas.

And this is where the refuelling begins.

## I Met Lee from PnP Games

I walk into the store, probably after most people have already picked up their copies, and see…

…Lee, from the PnP Games commercials!

![Lee stands in a doorway wearing a PnP Games hockey jersey in one of the store’s commercials.](./lee-from-pnpgames.png)

I regret not getting a picture with him in the PnP hockey jersey, but I told him I think they’re doing great work and that I appreciate the commercials he makes for the EPNtv episodes.

> For context, the original EPN episodes aired on actual TV, complete with commercial breaks. The PnP Games commercials fill those breaks and give the episodes that old-school TV feel.

We talked about how the commercials got started. There was no grand plan to min-max the marketing spend. They wanted to make something that fit the feel of the episodes.

What sort of commercials make sense for a video game TV show? Video game store commercials, of course.

That stuck with me. Sometimes you do something for your business simply because it _makes sense to do it_.

While I was mulling that over, Lee got me set up at the counter to pick up my copy of *Ocarina of Time*. I waited there for Victor Lucas to finish his conversation.

**Current Status: Intrigued**

## I Met Victor Lucas from Electric Playground

I’d wanted to meet Vic for quite a while. I’m a fan of his work, and someone once told me that if you enjoy someone’s work or it inspires you, you should tell them.

Of course, you can do that in an email, a comment, or a social media post. But my style is old school. I don’t consider the job done until I tell them to their face.

_This was that chance._

Once Vic was free, we had a great chat, one I won’t forget anytime soon. We talked about *Electric Playground* and *Reviews on the Run*, the move from network TV to online, his plans for the shows, being dads to daughters, and the optimism he brings to his work.

One thing stuck with me more than the rest. We were talking about making games and making TV shows, and he said we should keep making things because you never know who’s going to watch (or play) them. I’m paraphrasing, but that was the idea.

I’d heard that advice before. I already believed it. But this time it hit me like lightning.

Here’s a guy who invented video game review shows and still makes them because someone might watch one someday. Who knows what might happen after that?

It takes business sense as well as creativity to keep an independent show going as long as Vic has. And after all that time, the possibility of reaching someone still matters to him.

**Current Status: Inspired**

## TL;DR

Victor’s enthusiasm for what he does is infectious. Video game journalism is taking a beating alongside the rest of the industry, and he still appreciates that he gets to do this work.

Lee keeps making retro-style commercials for a store that sells physical games, even as the world tells him the future is all digital.

And yet they keep making and doing cool stuff, however they can. It’s hard work, and it probably won’t make them rich (though I’m sure they wouldn’t mind). They do it because _they can_.

That stuff they make? It keeps me connected to gaming while I juggle two kids and a day job. It also keeps me chasing a dream of making a game that someone like Victor might review someday, one you might even find in a store like the one we were chatting in today.

So I unwrapped my day-one special edition of *Ocarina of Time* and asked Vic to sign the sleeve. Now, I have a second autographed game in my video game collection thath I can pick up and hold and bring me back to this great conversation.

![Victor Lucas and David Wesst grin and point at each other inside PnP Games.](./IMG_3797%20Medium.jpeg)

## The Point

When the world feels backwards and upside down, we need the joy that comes from making things more than ever. When you try to make something, there’s no end to the things that can get in the way.

Life. Money. Work.

Don't worry about them and go make something.

After those conversations, I feel refresh and ready to keep chasing my dream of making a video game.

Because who knows who’ll play it one day, or where it could take me?

Play Forever.

> P.S. Special thanks to Vic and Lee. You got me excited to keep chasing the dream and making things. Who knows where it’ll lead?
